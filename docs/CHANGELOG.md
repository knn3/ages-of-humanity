# Changelog

What was built, in order, from the user's requests (newest last). Published artifact versions are listed in the artifact's history.

## Early development (before the tracker)
1. **The game**: a 2D side-scrolling simulation of civilisation from the Stone Age to the information age and beyond, where the player adds elements and events and peoples react. (Interstellar part deferred.)
2. **12 ages** (Prehistoric → Future), a bigger world with ocean between landmasses.
3. **Scroll to zoom** and **full screen** mode.
4. **Free drag** in all directions.
5. **Globe zoom**: zooming far out bends the world into a globe through the Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere and Exosphere into space.
6. **Nature**: forests, wildlife, soil and fish, and what peoples do to them (clearing, hunting, parks, rewilding, extinctions).
7. **Info redesign**: names only, with an **i** for details.
8. Hover only when zoomed in; underground deposits get info; **normal cursor**.

69. "Double check if the orbit of the Moon to the Earth physics exact?": it was not. The Moon went round in ~11 game days and the wrong way. 74. "Fix each planet's orbit direction and speed to follow real physics" (session task): three things were still wrong after 73.
   - **Directions were all reversed.** The flat map's Sun runs left to right, so the screen is a view from above the north pole (east is anticlockwise round the globe), yet the globe's Sun crossed right to left and every orbit, the Earth's spin and the Moon turned clockwise: a south-pole view, at odds with the flat map. Day and night on the globe follow the drawn Sun (`V.dayV`).
   - **The solar-system view spun once a game day.** The whole system turned with the sky at every zoom, so on screen every planet went round the Sun once a day (48 s) plus its own small motion: the speed ratios were meaningless and Mercury even ran backwards on screen. Up close the view still turns with the Earth; zoomed out it stops turning (`skyPh`/`updSky`, one sky angle at every zoom, blended by rate; the time of day is read back from the drawn Sun, so zooming alone never moves the Sun or the Moon and what the solar view showed is still there when you zoom back in).    - The Moon stays on the day clock (29.53-day synodic month, which with a 365.256-day year gives the 27.32-day sidereal month). The day (48 s) and the game year (60 s of sim time) are separate clocks by design, so the month and the year are not in their real ratio.
   - "The Moon in the close-up does not follow the zoomed-out view, fix it along with the Sun": the first version snapped the Sun (and the Moon with it) back to the time of day as you zoomed in (up to ~0.7 rad in one step).
